# Evidence, original writing, and reuse

The library explains ideas in independently written language. A source citation
supports a claim; it does not grant permission to reproduce the source's wording,
translate its prose, imitate its examples, or relicense its diagrams. Public
availability and a permissive code license do not establish a document's license.

## Authoring contract

1. Read the relevant primary material and identify the actual proposition it supports.
2. Record the publisher, version/commit, inspected URL, content hash, source license
   evidence, and limits of applicability. Retain raw material only in the private
   review workspace when permitted; do not commit it or upload it to public artifacts.
3. Explain the engineering decision in original wording, with independent structure
   and examples. Do not copy source sentences, translate them as new authorship, or
   produce a close paraphrase that preserves distinctive expression or examples.
4. Distinguish source-supported behavior from the library's own contextual synthesis.
   State assumptions, tradeoffs, change triggers, and how the recommendation can be
   checked or disproved. Link exact evidence rather than quoting it into a guide.
5. Review all four languages for the same obligations and caveats. Translate the
   library's own English text; do not translate upstream documentation into the library.
6. Inspect rights and originality before public transfer. Unknown source licenses
   mean reference-only use here, not a guessed right to copy. Escalate unresolved
   reuse questions to the rights holder or a qualified reviewer.

## What checks establish

The source monitor observes reachability, changed bytes, and review age. It does
not endorse a publisher, prove a claim, or accept a new baseline. Immutable reviewed
references make a past reading recoverable; mutable tracking URLs reveal later
changes. A review must also inspect substantive linked specifications when an index
only points to the real requirements.

Claim-to-source mappings document which passages support an engineering assertion
and which parts are local synthesis. They are a review aid, not a claim that every
sentence is independently proven. Source counts, word counts, matching revisions,
and a successful build do not measure the depth or correctness of the guidance.

A textual overlap screen can find suspicious exact sequences in its inspected
corpus. It cannot clear paraphrases, translations, unseen sources, images, trademarks,
patent issues, or all applicable rights. Record its inputs, exclusions, threshold,
and findings; never label a clean screen as legal clearance.

## Licenses and necessary third-party material

Original library prose and translations retain CC BY 4.0 even when exported inside
JSON. Original code/configuration and non-expressive metadata use MIT. Consult
LICENSE-CONTENT and LICENSE-CODE for exact scope. Contributors must hold the rights
needed to submit their contribution under the applicable terms.

Required third-party software licenses, notices, and corresponding source are
retained separately and keep their upstream licenses. They are not library advice
or relicensed as our MIT code. The site publishes a third-party notice inventory
because it redistributes theme/search software; that is separate from merely
linking to external documentation. Changes to dependencies or bundled assets require
a fresh license and notice review before publication.
